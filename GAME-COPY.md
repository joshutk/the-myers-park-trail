## 2. About the trail

ABOUT THE TRAIL

1. You are one of the pastors of Myers Park Presbyterian Church. Drive the church mini-bus from the early days meeting at the Queens College chapel all the way to Centennial Sunday in 2026.
2. Keep the church healthy by managing: the Funds, the Members, the congregation's Morale, Participation in the life of the church, and your own Stamina. When a bar runs out, your tenure ends.
3. Every so often, you'll have the opportunity to meet a big need in the life of the church and the community. To pay for it, bring in pledges by holding a stewardship drive.
4. The history is true, drawn from "A Legacy of Love" by Lois Stickell. A few small events are invented for fun.

## 11. Landmarks (scenes + "Did you know?" facts)

Each landmark shows a short scene, then an optional "Did you know?" card. Building-project landmarks (marked ‡) also run the ledger in section 14.

### 1926 — Queens College Chapel
**Scene:** Eighty-two people sign a petition for a new church in Myers Park. It's really happening!
**Did you know?:** On October 10, 1926, one hundred eighteen people met in the Queens College chapel and eighty-two of them signed a petition to organize a new Presbyterian church in Myers Park. By the time it was formally organized that November it already had two hundred forty-five members. · Myers Park was then a brand-new streetcar suburb of tree-lined curving streets. The founders had no building, no lot, and, as one of them put it, no funds for either. They were excited to get going anyway. *(p.6-8)*

### 1927 ‡ — Building the First Church (Founders Campaign)
**Scene:** The first pastor sees the new lot "fit only for broom-straw," sinks into a chair, and asks: "What in the world?"
**Did you know?:** The Founders Campaign raised about two hundred seventy-five thousand dollars for the stone Sanctuary and the Sunday School classrooms behind it. The architects gave it an English Gothic look, and the stone was hauled from a quarry at the edge of the Myers Park Country Club by mule. · Stone kept disappearing from the construction site overnight, so the church hired a night watchman. *(p.19-34, 433)*

### 1929 — First Service in the Sanctuary
**Scene:** The first worship service held on the church's own property.
**Did you know?:** The first service in the stone Sanctuary was held in April 1929 on a very warm Sunday. There was no air conditioning, the temperature was in the eighties. One founding member still pronounced it very, very comfortable. · Air conditioning did not arrive until 1952. *(p.38)*

### 1931 — The Depression Reaches the Pews
**Scene:** The stock market has crashed. The church has a loan out on the Sanctuary, and many of its members are hit hard.
**Did you know?:** Four of Charlotte's seven banks failed during the Depression, and contributions to the church fell with them. In 1932 the Session cut the pay of the paid choir members by a quarter. · None of the singers quit, and benevolences were not reduced. They also proceeded with construction of the manse the church had promised its first pastor, albeit four years behind schedule. *(p.52-55)*

### 1941 — A Church on the Home Front
**Scene:** The new Educational Building opens. The Second World War begins soon after. Many members go off to serve.
**Did you know?:** A 1940 campaign raised a hundred and fifty thousand dollars for a three-story Educational Building (the B Building today) — Sunday-School classes had been crowded and they needed more space. It opened in late 1941, just as the United States entered the Second World War. · The church was named a civil-defense welfare station. In 1944, German prisoners of war were brought to Myers Park to clear the pine trees along what is now Hampton Avenue. *(p.68, 73, 86-89)*

### 1944 — The Noon Prayer Bell
**Scene:** The bell rings at noon for the troops. German prisoners of war clear the pines out front.
**Did you know?:** Beginning in May 1944 the church rang its bell every day at noon and asked the congregation, wherever they were, to stop and pray for the men and women overseas. The church kept a Servicemen's Bulletin to stay in touch with those who had shipped out. · On D-Day, the building was left open all day so people could come in and pray. *(p.86-90)*

### 1952 — The Children's Building
**Scene:** The new Children's Building opens! Session air-conditions the Sanctuary in your absence. Congratulations.
**Did you know?:** A year earlier, the church's twenty-fifth-anniversary campaign had raised three hundred thousand dollars — a hundred and eighty thousand to build a new Children's Building (the C Building today), and the rest to help start churches across Charlotte. The building opened around 1952. · That same year, while pastor James A. Jones was away touring global mission sites including Congo, the officers voted to air-condition the Sanctuary — reasoning it would boost summer attendance. Jones found out when he got home. *(p.114, 119, 122-123)*

### 1959 ‡ — Expanding the Sanctuary (Sanctuary Expansion)
**Scene:** Space in the original Sanctuary is getting tight! Time to expand.
**Did you know?:** The 1959 campaign raised over a million dollars. It lengthened the Sanctuary by two windows' worth of pews and added an Education and Fellowship Building (the D Building today). The front wall of the Sanctuary was supposed to come down with a wrecking ball — but the ball bounced off the granite, and the crew finished the job with hammers and chisels. *(p.138-144, 433)*

### 1962 — Rededication
**Scene:** The Sanctuary reopens with a new Casavant organ organ and Willett stained glass raredos.
**Did you know?:** The expanded Sanctuary was rededicated in March 1962. It had a new thirty-six-hundred-pipe organ and, behind the communion table, a fancy new reredos. It was also reconfigured from a two-aisle layout to a single center aisle. · The stained-glass windows replaced the old clear glass over the course of the 1960s. *(p.142-144)*

### 1971 — First Women Elders
**Scene:** The first women are ordained as church elders.
**Did you know?:** In November 1971 Lib Harkey and Danny Verner were ordained as the church's first women elders, a year after the first women deacons. The denomination had voted to ordain women as elders in 1964. · One member objected, saying that the women's high heels would clatter on the stone floor as they served communion. Lib Harkey made sure she wore her heels. *(p.179-180)*

### 1976 — 50th Anniversary
**Scene:** A jubilant celebration, including a "Saints and Sinners" roast.
**Did you know?:** The fiftieth anniversary in May 1976 included a Saints and Sinners evening where the congregation roasted itself. The new pastor, J. Randolph Taylor, was teased for not preaching enough hellfire. · One member played Lucifer and sang a number that included the line "Down with virtue, down with goodness, sin is here to stay!" *(p.197-198)*

### 1983 — Reunion in Atlanta
**Scene:** The Southern and Northern churches are reunited to become the PC(USA).
**Did you know?:** In June 1983 the northern and southern Presbyterian churches reunited at the General Assembly in Atlanta after one hundred twenty-two years apart, a split that dated to the Civil War. Eight thousand Presbyterians marched through downtown behind bagpipes. · Myers Park's pastor, J. Randolph Taylor, was elected the first Moderator of the reunited church. His duties as Moderator would mean significant time away from the church. *(p.223-225)*

### 1989 ‡ — Building the Christian Life Center (New Horizons)
**Scene:** Building a gym and dedicated office space for pastors and staff.
**Did you know?:** The New Horizons campaign raised about six million dollars for the Christian Life Center, a new slate roof, and an elevator that the children promptly tied up riding up and down. The thirty-thousand-square-foot building added a gym and meeting space the church had wanted for years. · Hurricane Hugo tore through Charlotte in September 1989, downing eighty thousand trees and leaving most of the city without power. The church held a relief breakfast and broke ground on the new building about a week later. *(p.256-263, 434)*

### 1996 — Room in the Inn
**Scene:** Neighbors without homes sleep in the church. A house rolls down Providence Road at midnight.
**Did you know?:** Room in the Inn began at the church in December 1996, offering people without a home a warm place to sleep through the winter. It continues today. · In 1997, the lot across Providence Road was purchased by the church. The house that stood on it was picked up and moved down Providence Road at midnight to a new location. *(p.287-289, 342)*

### 2002 — Celebrate Launches
**Scene:** A new worship service begins in the gym during an interim time between head of staff pastors.
**Did you know?:** The Celebrate service began in February 2002 in the Christian Life Center gym. It was a more casual, contemporary service than the one in the Sanctuary. Tarps were spread on the gym floor and a video feed carried the sermon. · About three hundred people came to the first one. The organizers had hoped it would bring in people the traditional service was not reaching. *(p.312-314)*

### 2008 ‡ — Building the Outreach Center (Deeper Discipleship)
**Scene:** The Great Recession hits as the church sets out to raise millions of dollars.
**Did you know?:** The Deeper Discipleship campaign raised twenty-seven million dollars even as the 2008 financial crisis unfolded around it. It built the Outreach Center, turned the Christian Life Center into classrooms, Oxford Hall, and the Welcome Center, paid off three million dollars of debt, and invested significantly in our Outreach partnerships. · That December, the church still needed one point six million dollars for the annual budget. One cost-cutting idea on the table was to stop giving away coffee, saving about seventeen thousand dollars a year. *(p.331-340, 356, 434)*

### 2011 — The Outreach Center Opens
**Scene:** A new building across the road.
**Did you know?:** The Outreach Center was dedicated in June 2011. It added a wellness center, meeting rooms, and space for the church's growing outreach ministries. · The church had planned for about one hundred fifty Wellness Ministry memberships. Five hundred seventy-six people signed up. *(p.353, 356)*

### 2015 — The Wedding Policy
**Scene:** The denomination has changed its definition of marriage to include people of the same gender. Session holds a town hall.
**Did you know?:** In 2015, after the denomination changed its definition of marriage, the Session held a town hall, several listening sessions, and plenty of meetings to determine a path forward. They ultimately amended the church's wedding policy to include people of all genders. Some families left and annual giving declined. *(p.367-368)*

### 2020 ‡ — Renovating the Children's Buildings (Cultivate)
**Scene:** A new campaign launched in the middle of a pandemic.
**Did you know?:** The Cultivate campaign was approved in December 2019 and launched in 2021, in the middle of the COVID-19 pandemic. It renovated the Weekday School, children's ministry spaces, Providence Hall, Cornerstone House, and the columbarium. It also funded a connector building between the C and D buildings and invested heavily in affordable housing and housing stability programs. · When worship first moved online due to COVID, a Sunday might have thirty-three people in the building and nine hundred fifty watching from home. A Zoom morning prayer started then and continues today. *(p.394-400, 434)*

### 2026 — Centennial Sunday
**Scene:** One hundred years. You made it!
**Did you know?:** Centennial week: November 1 through 8, 2026. The church that started in 1926 with two hundred forty-five members and no building reported four thousand seven hundred thirty-five members in 2024. *(p.419)*

**Did you know?:** The Wall Street crash of October 1929 wiped out fortunes and tipped the United States into the Great Depression. The church had just signed a mortgage on its new Sanctuary, and Charlotte's banks and givers were badly hurt in the years that followed.

**1927** — The deed to the lot carries a racial covenant restricting the land to white owners. It was common in Myers Park, and it was wrong. *(p.19)*
**Did you know?:** Racially restrictive covenants -- deed clauses barring the sale of a lot to non-white owners -- were common across American suburbs in the early twentieth century, and Myers Park was planned with them. The U.S. Supreme Court ruled such covenants legally unenforceable in 1948, and the Fair Housing Act outlawed them in 1968.
